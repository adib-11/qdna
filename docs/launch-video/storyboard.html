01

Creative brief

Drop in the past papers you already have. Get a ranked, auditable answer to what to study first.

  • ObjectiveLaunch qDNA to students and to the people who recommend tools to them: course reps, seniors, teaching assistants. Drive visits to the live app and first runs on a bundled sample.
  • AudienceAn engineering undergraduate three weeks before a final. Has a folder of scanned past papers, a group chat full of rumours about "important questions", and no time.
  • InsightStudents already believe questions repeat. Nobody has shown them how much, which ones, and on what evidence.
  • Reasons to believeReal corpus, real numbers: 154 questions across 7 papers. 22 of 57 topics cover 70.8% of every question. Hiding the 2024 final, 11 of its 24 questions had been asked before in another form.
  • ToneCalm, exact, a little dry. The senior who has seen every paper, not an announcer. Confidence comes from the numbers, never from adjectives.
  • MandatoriesProduct name and URL. "Evidence-weighted, never a forecast." Own-key model: pages go from the browser to Gemini on the student's key, nothing is uploaded to qDNA's server. Samples work with no key.
  • Never say"Predict", "guaranteed", "leaked", "AI tells you what's on the exam", "offline", "your files never leave your device" (pages do go to Gemini).
  • SuccessViewer can say in one sentence what qDNA does and why to trust it. Click-through to the live app; sample runs in the first 48 hours.
02

Narrative arc & beat map

Act 1, The pile (0–16 s). Recognition. The viewer sees their own folder of papers, then the overwhelm of 154 questions on ink black. The title lands on clean paper: the first breath.

Act 2, The genome (16–51 s). The machine at work, shown not told. Pages go in, a question is fully read, the genome builds year by year, the Pareto line crosses 70%, a question returns in new clothes.

Act 3, The answer (51–80 s). Payoff, then proof. The study plan draws itself in highlighter. A hidden paper is revealed to test it. Practice and hints, then the end card.

Music at 96 BPM · one bar = 2.5 s · 32 bars · ● hit points are cut or reveal frames that land on a downbeat

  • HighlighterA background band at 64% of the line height, sitting low (86%), drawn left to right in 10–16 frames on power2.inOut. Its length always equals the value it marks.
  • Red penClip reveal left to right, 12 frames. Only ever on marks.
  • Type entrancesWord or line masks, rising 105% on expo.out. Never fade-in alone, never letter-spacing tweens.
  • CutsHard cuts on downbeats. One match-cut per act boundary. No dissolves, no whooshes, no 3D.
  • Texture9% static paper grain, multiply. Locked, never boiling.
05

Sound design & mix

  • MusicOriginal score, synthesised for this film, so no licence is needed. 96 BPM in F major, a plucked marimba motif over Fmaj7–Dm9–B♭maj7–Csus. A dissonant cluster swells under scene 02 and cuts dead on the title. Kick and bass enter at 04, a pad lifts at 06, the band is full at 09, drops to the motif alone for the backtest flip, a low hit lands on “11”, and an Fmaj9 rings out on the end card.
  • VoiceFish Audio s2.1-pro, library voice “Energetic Male” (young, bright, ad read). Twelve lines, 63 s of speech, each placed on its scene and levelled to the same loudness. For broadcast, re-record with a human voice to the same timings.
  • No-VO cutSame timing, VO lines burned in as captions, for muted autoplay feeds.
  • PaperSheet drops (01), rustle bed (02), card flip (10).
  • Marking toolsFelt-tip squeak on every highlighter stroke, nib scratch on every red mark.
  • DataSoft ticks for counters and chips, pitched up by year in 06. A single bell on the 70% crossing.
  • MixMeasured: −14.2 LUFS integrated, −1.2 dBTP (BS.1770, 4× oversampled). Music ducks 6 dB under the voice. Stems for voice, music and effects ship beside the mix in audio/; build_audio.py rebuilds all of it. Broadcast version at −23 LUFS (EBU R128) on request.

Production plan & sign-off

  • PipelineRemotion compositions in a video/ workspace beside the app. Scenes import the app's tokens and run analyze() on data/swe.json, so every number on screen is computed by product code, and a data change re-renders the film. Fonts are the app's own (Epilogue, IBM Plex Mono, both OFL).
  • Hand-off optionIf a motion studio takes it in After Effects: this animatic as the timing reference, genome and Pareto data exported as CSV, the claims register as the lock list.
  • AssetsScans of the seven SWE papers from papers/ for scenes 01 and 04. One paper-grain plate. Licensed music bed. VO recording.
